# Poultry: broilers, layers, improved kienyeji (Kenya)

## Poultry brooding (chicks, first 4 weeks)
- Brooder temperature: 32–35°C in week 1, then lower about 3°C each week until about 21°C by week 4–5.
- Read the chicks: huddled under the heat = too cold; spread to the edges, panting, wings out = too hot; evenly spread and active = right.
- Clean water must be ready before chicks arrive; keep litter dry (wood shavings), and avoid draughts while keeping ventilation.
- Space at market age: about 8–10 broilers per m². Layers on deep litter: about 4–5 birds per m².

## Broiler growth targets and feed
Reference targets (Ross 308, as-hatched, Aviagen 2022), well-managed flock:
- Day 7: 213 g · Day 14: 533 g · Day 21: 1.01 kg · Day 28: 1.62 kg · Day 35: 2.30 kg · Day 42: 3.00 kg.
- Cumulative feed per bird: about 2.05 kg by day 28 and 3.2 kg by day 35; FCR about 1.27 at day 28 and 1.40 at day 35.
- Smallholder Kenyan flocks usually grow slower; birds 10–20% below these targets are common, more than 25% below means a feed, heat, water or disease problem.
- Feed broiler starter (crumbs) first, then finisher; switch around day 21 (or as the feed maker advises). Rule of thumb in Kenya: about 1 kg starter then about 2.5 kg finisher per bird to market.
- Mortality rule of thumb: aim for under 5% for the whole cycle; investigate anything above that.

## Layer rearing and egg production
- Feeds: chick mash (0–8 weeks), growers mash (8–18 weeks), layers mash from point of lay.
- Point of lay: about 18–20 weeks for commercial layers; improved kienyeji usually start at 5–6 months.
- Feed in lay: about 110–130 g per hen per day; water about twice the feed weight (more in hot weather).
- Production drops are commonly caused by: heat or cold stress, feed change or poor feed, short daylight, missed vaccination (Newcastle/IB), worms or mites, moulting, and old age of the flock.
- Improved kienyeji (e.g. KALRO improved, Kenbro, Kuroiler, Sasso): dual-purpose; egg numbers depend heavily on feeding; free-range birds lay far less than confined, fully fed birds.

## Poultry vaccination schedule
Always confirm with your vet or the hatchery's programme; local disease risk changes the schedule. Ask the hatchery which vaccines the chicks already received.
Broilers (Kenchic vet guidance, Standard):
- Day 1 at hatchery: Marek's; often also Gumboro, Newcastle (ND) and infectious bronchitis (IB).
- Day 12–14: ND + IB booster (eye drop or drinking water).
- If Gumboro was not given at the hatchery: day 14 with a booster a week later (KALRO via The Star).
Layers and improved kienyeji:
- Hatchery: Marek's (often ND, IB, Gumboro too).
- ND: about week 1 and week 3, then boosters every about 3 months for life (KALRO).
- Gumboro: repeat in the first weeks (about day 14 and day 21).
- Fowl pox: about 5–6 weeks, wing-web stab.
- Fowl typhoid: about 6–8 weeks, injection.
- In lay: ND + IB boosters about every 3 months.
Drinking-water vaccination tips: use clean non-chlorinated water, withhold water 1–2 hours first so birds are thirsty, give in the cool morning, let birds finish it within about 2 hours, and never mix with disinfectants or medicines.

## Poultry diseases (signs, action)
- Newcastle disease (Kideri/Mdondo): twisted neck, green diarrhoea, paralysis, sudden deaths. No treatment; vaccinate. Many deaths fast → report to the county vet.
- Gumboro (IBD): chicks 3–6 weeks, dull, white watery droppings, dirty vent, sudden deaths. No cure; supportive care, vet may advise antibiotics for secondary infection; vaccinate.
- Coccidiosis: bloody or brown droppings, ruffled, huddled birds; damp litter and overcrowding. Treat promptly with an anticoccidial from the agrovet/vet; dry the litter.
- Fowl typhoid: green-yellow diarrhoea, pale comb and wattles, drop in eggs, mostly adults. Isolate, vet-directed antibiotics for the flock, vaccinate.
- Pullorum: chicks 1–3 weeks, white pasty vent, passed through eggs from carrier hens. Buy chicks from certified hatcheries.
- Fowl pox: warty scabs on comb, wattles, eyes; mouth lesions. No cure; vaccinate, control mosquitoes.
- Chronic respiratory disease (CRD/mycoplasma): sneezing, rattling breathing, slow growth; worse with ammonia, damp, crowding. Improve ventilation; vet for treatment.
- Infectious bronchitis: coughing, drop in eggs, thin or wrinkled shells. Vaccinate.
- Avian influenza: many sudden deaths, swollen purple head/comb, sharp egg drop. Report immediately; do not move or sell birds.
- Worms: weight loss, pale comb, poor laying. Deworm from about 8 weeks, then every 3 months; rotate dewormer types.
- Mites/lice/fleas: feather loss, pale birds, scratching. Treat birds and house with a poultry acaricide; clean nests and perches.
- Cannibalism/pecking: overcrowding, too much light/heat, low protein or salt. Give space, dim light, check feed.

## Poultry biosecurity
- One entry with a footbath; change shoes/clothes; limit visitors and other farmers' crates.
- All-in, all-out per house; clean, disinfect and rest the house 1–2 weeks between lots.
- Keep wild birds, rodents and dogs out; remove dead birds daily and burn or bury them deep.
- Buy chicks only from known hatcheries; don't mix ages or new birds with old ones without quarantine.
- Keep records of deaths, feed and vaccinations: in Shamba Secure use "Record a loss" on the lot and the lot's reminders.
