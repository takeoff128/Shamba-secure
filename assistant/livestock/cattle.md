# Cattle: dairy and beef (Kenya)

## Dairy cow feeding
- Dry matter: a milking cow needs at least 3% of body weight in dry matter daily (600 kg cow ≈ 18 kg DM ≈ 60 kg of fresh 70%-moisture fodder) (KALRO scientist, Standard 2022).
- Water: 80–120 litres a day for an adult dairy cow, more in heat and high yield; keep it clean and always available.
- Minerals: about 100–200 g per cow per day, or as the product label says (mineral lick or powder).
- Concentrates: common Kenyan rule of thumb is about 1 kg dairy meal per 2 litres of milk above what the forage alone supports (often taken as above 5–7 litres); split into several feeds a day.
- Good forages: Napier, Brachiaria, Boma Rhodes, lucerne, desmodium, sweet potato vines, maize/sorghum silage. Chop Napier; wilt wet legumes before feeding to avoid bloat.
- Sudden milk drop: check water access, feed change, heat stress, mastitis, lameness, heat (oestrus), or illness (fever).

## Calf rearing
- Colostrum within the first 2 hours of birth, about 10% of body weight (3–4 litres for a Friesian calf) in the first feed; the gut absorbs antibodies poorly after 12–24 hours.
- Milk about 10% of body weight per day split in 2 feeds; offer calf pellets, clean water and good hay from week 1–2.
- Wean at about 3 months when the calf eats concentrate well. Dip or disinfect the navel at birth.

## Cattle breeding
- Heat cycle 21 days (18–24); heat lasts about 12–18 hours. Signs: standing to be mounted (key sign), clear mucus, bellowing, restlessness, mounting others.
- AM–PM rule: heat seen in the morning → serve in the evening; seen in the evening → serve next morning.
- Gestation about 283 days (about 9 months 10 days). Dry off about 60 days before calving.
- Target calving interval: 12–13 months; first service for heifers by weight (about 60–65% of adult weight), not age alone.

## Cattle diseases (signs, action)
- East Coast Fever (ECF): spread by the brown ear tick; swollen lymph node below the ear, high fever, fast breathing, froth at the nose, cough; very deadly in exotic and young cattle. Call a vet early (treatment works best early). Prevent with regular tick control and the ITM ("Muguga cocktail") vaccine given by trained vets.
- Anaplasmosis (gall sickness): fever, pale or yellow gums, constipation, weakness. Tick-borne; vet treatment.
- Babesiosis (redwater): fever, red/coffee-coloured urine, anaemia. Tick-borne; vet treatment.
- Heartwater: fever, nervous signs, circling, sudden death. Tick-borne (bont tick).
- Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD): blisters in the mouth, feet and teats, drooling, lameness, sharp milk drop. No treatment; vaccinate every 6 months in at-risk areas. Must be reported; follow movement restrictions.
- Lumpy skin disease (LSD): firm skin nodules all over, fever, swollen lymph nodes, milk drop. Vaccinate yearly; control biting flies. Report to the vet.
- Anthrax: sudden death, bloody discharge from nose, mouth and anus, blood that does not clot. Do NOT open, skin, sell or eat the carcass; report immediately. Yearly vaccination in risk areas. Dangerous to people.
- Blackquarter: young cattle 6 months–2 years, hot swollen crackling muscles (often a leg), lameness, sudden death. Yearly vaccination.
- Brucellosis: abortion in late pregnancy, retained afterbirth. Infects people: boil milk, use gloves at calving. Vaccinate heifer calves at 3–8 months (S19) with the vet.
- Rift Valley Fever (RVF): abortion storms and young-stock deaths after heavy rains. Infects people. Vaccinate before the rains in risk areas (the live vaccine can cause abortion in pregnant animals; ask the vet); report.
- Mastitis: hot, swollen or hard quarter, clots or watery milk. Use the CMT (California Mastitis Test), milk infected cows last, clean and dry teats, post-milking teat dip, dry-cow therapy with the vet. Keep milk out of sale for the drug's withdrawal period.
- Milk fever: within about 48 hours of calving, high-yielding cows: staggering, cold ears, lying with head turned to the flank. EMERGENCY: call the vet now (needs calcium into the vein).
- Bloat: swelling high on the LEFT side, distress. EMERGENCY: stop the feed, keep the cow walking, call the vet. Prevent by wilting legumes and not turning hungry cattle onto lush legume pasture.
- Worms and liver fluke: rough coat, poor growth, diarrhoea, bottle jaw. Deworm young stock regularly and at the start and end of the rains; flukicide in wet/swampy areas.
- CBPP (contagious bovine pleuropneumonia): cough, laboured breathing, standing with elbows out, fever; mostly in pastoral areas. Vaccine exists; must be reported.
- Trypanosomiasis (nagana): weight loss, anaemia, fever in tsetse areas. Vet treatment; tsetse control.

## Cattle vaccination calendar (typical, confirm locally)
- FMD: every 6 months in risk areas (often in government campaigns).
- LSD: yearly (Kenya uses an LSD Neethling vaccine or a sheep/goat-pox-strain vaccine; ask the vet).
- Anthrax and blackquarter: yearly (combined vaccines exist).
- Brucellosis: once, heifer calves 3–8 months.
- RVF: before the long rains in risk areas when advised. The live RVF vaccine can cause abortion: ask the vet before vaccinating pregnant animals.
- ECF (ITM): once, by a trained vet; tick control still needed.
