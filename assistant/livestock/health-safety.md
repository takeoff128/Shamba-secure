# Animal health safety rules

## When to call a vet immediately
- Several animals or birds dying suddenly or within a day or two.
- Any sudden death with bleeding from the nose, mouth or anus (possible anthrax: don't open the carcass).
- Pigs with red/purple skin and deaths (possible ASF); many birds dying with twisted necks or swollen heads (Newcastle or avian influenza).
- A cow down after calving (milk fever), a swollen left side (bloat), difficult birth longer than about 30–60 minutes of strong straining.
- Animals with strange behaviour, aggression or drooling and difficulty swallowing (possible rabies: keep people away).
- Mouth or foot blisters in cattle, goats, sheep or pigs (possible FMD).
- Suspected poisoning (after acaricide use, mouldy/aflatoxin feed or toxic plants): trembling, drooling, collapse, sudden deaths after a feed change. Remove the feed or source and call a vet.
- Prolapse (womb or rectum), choke (drooling, can't swallow, bloating), or heat stroke (panting, collapse in hot weather): call a vet; move to shade and give water for heat stress.

## Diseases to report to the government vet
Report suspected cases to the County Director of Veterinary Services or the nearest vet office, and don't move or sell animals meanwhile: foot-and-mouth disease, African swine fever, anthrax, Rift Valley fever, rabies, PPR, CCPP, CBPP, lumpy skin disease, sheep and goat pox, Newcastle disease, avian influenza.

## Medicines, withdrawal periods and agrovets
- Use medicines only as the label or a registered vet/animal health professional directs; finish the course.
- Respect withdrawal periods on the label: no milk, eggs or meat for sale or eating until it has passed.
- Don't use antibiotics to make animals grow faster or to replace hygiene and vaccination.
- Store vaccines in a cold chain (fridge, not freezer); once mixed, use within 1–2 hours and throw away what is left.
- The assistant does not give drug doses: dosing depends on weight, product and diagnosis.

## Diseases that infect people (zoonoses)
- Anthrax, brucellosis, Rift Valley fever, rabies, bovine TB, avian influenza, leptospirosis, Q fever, erysipelas, pork tapeworm (cysticercosis), orf, salmonella.
- Boil milk, wear gloves at births and abortions, don't eat animals that died of unknown causes, wash hands after handling animals.
- Animal bite (possible rabies): wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes and go to a health facility the same day for post-exposure vaccine.
- Anyone who handled a suspect anthrax carcass and gets skin sores or fever should see a doctor.
