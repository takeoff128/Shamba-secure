# Goats and sheep (Kenya)

## Goat and sheep breeds and milk
- Dairy goats (KALRO): Saanen 2–5 L/day, Toggenburg 2–4 L, German/French Alpine 2–4 L, British Alpine about 2–4.5 L. Crosses with local goats give less but are hardier.
- Meat goats: Galla, Boer and crosses, Small East African. Sheep: Dorper, Red Maasai, crosses.

## Goat and sheep breeding
- Goats: heat cycle about 21 days (18–22); heat lasts 12–48 hours (average 36). Serve about 24 hours after heat starts, or every 12 hours while she stands (KALRO).
- Sheep: heat cycle about 17 days (14–19); heat lasts about 24–36 hours.
- Goat heat signs: restlessness, red swollen vulva, mucus, tail wagging, bleating, frequent urination.
- Gestation about 150 days (145–155) for both goats and sheep. Does can kid about 1.5 times a year.
- First mating: when the female reaches about two-thirds of adult weight (often 8–12 months), not by age alone.
- Kids and lambs: colostrum in the first hours; wean at about 3 months.

## Goat and sheep diseases (signs, action)
- PPR (peste des petits ruminants, "goat plague"): fever, discharge from eyes and nose, mouth sores, diarrhoea, pneumonia, many deaths. A national priority disease: report and vaccinate (government campaigns).
- CCPP (contagious caprine pleuropneumonia, goats): coughing, laboured breathing, fever, deaths. Vaccinate; isolate sick goats; vet-directed antibiotics.
- Sheep and goat pox: fever, skin nodules/scabs. Vaccinate.
- Combination vaccine: a "Combo vaccine kit" covering CCPP, sheep and goat pox and PPR was launched in Kenya in April 2026 (GALVmed / JOVAC); ask your vet whether it is available locally.
- Enterotoxaemia (pulpy kidney): sudden death in the best-fed young animals, often after a feed change. Vaccinate (two doses first time, then boosters as the label says); change feed gradually.
- Worms (especially Haemonchus/wireworm): pale eyelids and gums, bottle jaw, weakness, deaths after rains. Check eyelid colour; deworm the pale ones; rotate pasture; don't under-dose.
- Foot rot: lameness, smelly rotten hoof. Trim, footbath, keep pens dry; vet for antibiotics if bad.
- Orf: scabs on lips and mouth of kids; usually heals in weeks; can infect people's hands (wear gloves).
- Pneumonia: cough, nasal discharge, fever; worse in cold, damp, crowded housing.
- Mastitis (dairy goats): hot, swollen udder, abnormal milk. Clean milking; vet treatment; respect milk withdrawal.
- Coccidiosis (kids): diarrhoea, poor growth in crowded, wet pens.
- Bloat: swollen left side after lush legumes; emergency.
- Heartwater: tick-borne, nervous signs and sudden death. Tick control.
- Brucellosis: abortions; infects people; boil milk.
- Rift Valley fever (RVF): sheep and goats are the most affected: abortion storms and deaths of young lambs and kids after heavy rains. Infects people. Report; vaccinate before the rains in risk areas (the live vaccine can cause abortion; ask the vet about pregnant animals).
