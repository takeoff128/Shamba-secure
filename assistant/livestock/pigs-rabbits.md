# Pigs and rabbits (Kenya)

## Pig breeding and growth
- Breeds: Large White (common in Kenya), Landrace, Duroc, Hampshire, and crosses such as Camborough.
- Heat cycle about 21 days; heat lasts 2–3 days. Serve on the second day of heat and again 12–24 hours later.
- Gestation 114 days ("3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days"). Litter about 8–12 piglets; sows can farrow about twice a year.
- Sows return to heat about 3–7 days after weaning.
- Market: porkers about 60–80 kg; baconers about 90–100 kg at roughly 6–8 months with good feeding.

## Pig feeding and piglet care
- Feeds by stage: creep feed (piglets from about 2 weeks), sow and weaner meal, grower/finisher meal, lactating sow feed. Adult pigs eat about 2–4 kg a day; always give clean water.
- Piglets: keep them warm (about 30–32°C at birth via a heat lamp or creep area), make sure each suckles colostrum in the first hours, iron injection around day 3 if they are kept on concrete, wean at about 6–8 weeks on most smallholder farms.
- Deworm regularly (often every 2–3 months) and treat mange.
- Never feed uncooked swill or kitchen waste: cook it thoroughly (DVS advises boiling with about 2% magadi soda / soda ash) — this is how African swine fever spreads.

## Pig diseases (signs, action)
- African swine fever (ASF): high fever, not eating, red or purple skin on ears, belly and legs, bloody diarrhoea, sudden deaths, up to 100% of the herd. There is no commercially available vaccine for the ASF strains in Kenya, and no treatment (DVS). Report sick or dead pigs to the nearest vet office immediately; don't sell, move or slaughter. 2026 outbreaks were reported in Kiambu (Juja, Thika, Ruiru), Tharaka-Nithi, Busia, Embu, Kakamega, Siaya and Migori. ASF does not infect people.
- Prevention of ASF: restrict entry of people, pigs, vehicles and equipment; footbaths; buy pigs only from known farms and isolate new ones; cook swill; dispose of carcasses under vet guidance.
- Piglet scours (diarrhoea): keep pens warm and dry; vet for treatment; dehydration kills quickly.
- Mange: scratching, crusty skin; treat the whole herd.
- Pneumonia: coughing, poor growth; improve ventilation, avoid draughts and crowding.
- Worms: poor growth, rough coat; deworm.
- Anaemia in piglets: pale, weak, fast breathing at 2–3 weeks; prevent with iron.
- Erysipelas: fever, diamond-shaped red skin patches; vet treatment; vaccine exists. Can infect people through skin cuts.
- Pork tapeworm (cysticercosis): cysts in pork; infects people and can cause epilepsy. Confine pigs, use latrines, have meat inspected, cook pork well.

## Rabbit management
- Common breeds: New Zealand White, Californian, Flemish Giant, Chinchilla, Dutch and crosses.
- Feeding: rabbit pellets plus good hay or dry fodder; wilt greens before feeding and avoid wet, mouldy or freshly cut greens straight from the farm (KALRO). Clean water always.
- Housing: clean, dry, well-ventilated hutches; avoid crowding, cold, damp and direct sun.
- Breeding: does usually from about 5–6 months, bucks a little later; gestation about 31 days (28–32); litter about 6–8; give a nest box a few days before kindling; wean at about 6–8 weeks.
- Market: about 2–2.5 kg live weight at around 3–4 months.

## Rabbit diseases (signs, action)
- Coccidiosis: diarrhoea (may be bloody), pot belly, weight loss, rough coat. Hygiene, no crowding, clean feed/water; treatment in water from the vet/agrovet.
- Pasteurellosis ("snuffles"): sneezing, nasal discharge, watery eyes, head tilt; quarantine new rabbits 1–2 weeks; vet antibiotics; cull animals that don't respond.
- Pneumonia: breathing difficulty, nasal mucus, not eating; avoid cold, damp hutches.
- Ear canker (ear mites): crusts in ears, head shaking. Soften crusts with oil; vet/agrovet treatment.
- Skin mange: scaly skin, hair loss, itching. Isolate, clean hutches, treat.
- Salmonellosis/scours: diarrhoea, weight loss. Hygiene, quarantine; vet antibiotics. Never give rabbits oral penicillin, amoxicillin, ampicillin or lincomycin (fatal gut disease); use only antibiotics a vet confirms are safe for rabbits.
- Bloat and enteritis: swollen belly, watery diarrhoea, sudden death; often from too many fresh greens or mouldy feed.
- Weepy eye: red, watery eyes from dust or bacteria; eye wash and vet ointment.
